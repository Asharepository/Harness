Why we roast this way

Small systems, careful sourcing, and copy-worthy cups.

We work with importers and estates that share harvest data, moisture readings, and processing notes so our roast decisions are based on evidence instead of guesswork.

Traceable lots

Each bag lists producer, process, harvest window, and roast date because transparency should be standard, not premium. We want you to know what changed when a coffee tastes brighter in January than it did in November.

Profiled for sweetness

Our roast curves aim for soluble sweetness first, then texture, then aromatic lift. That means fewer smoky notes, more fruit definition, and a cup that stays balanced across espresso, immersion, and filter methods.

Roasted for Goa weather

Humidity changes extraction. We rest coffees with local conditions in mind and share grinder and ratio guidance that helps home brewers adapt when monsoon air makes yesterday’s recipe feel suddenly different.

Brew guide

Dial in three dependable recipes.

V60 for clarity

15 g dose250 g water2:45 target

Use 15 g coffee to 250 g water at 94°C, medium grind. Bloom with 45 g for 35 seconds, then pour in three gentle circles to finish by 2:10. Aim for a total drawdown around 2:45. This recipe highlights florals and citrus without making the cup feel thin. If the finish tastes sharp, grind slightly finer and pour a touch slower. If it tastes woody, coarsen the grind and raise the water temperature by one degree.

AeroPress for sweetness

16 g dose230 g water2:00 total

Use 16 g coffee to 230 g water at 92°C, medium-fine grind, inverted method. Add all water, stir five times, cap, and steep for 1:30 before flipping. Press slowly for 30 seconds. This gives a dense, sweet cup with enough body for milk or ice. For fruit-forward coffees, shorten the steep by 10 seconds. For chocolate-heavy coffees, extend the press slightly to pull more texture into the final cup.

French Press for body

30 g dose500 g water9:00 settle

Use 30 g coffee to 500 g water at 93°C, coarse grind. Pour all water, stir once, and steep for four minutes. Break the crust, skim the surface, and let the grounds settle for another five minutes before plunging halfway. This slower finish produces a cleaner press pot with less sludge and more definition. If the cup feels muddy, grind coarser. If it feels weak, increase dose before changing time.

Roast calendar

A weekly rhythm built for freshness, not warehouse stock.

We roast filter profiles on Tuesdays and Fridays, espresso profiles on Wednesdays and Saturdays, then cup every batch before it leaves the room. That cadence keeps our menu lively while giving each coffee enough rest to taste settled rather than frantic.

Tuesday

Filter-focused roasts for floral and washed coffees, packed by afternoon for local pickup and next-day dispatch.

Wednesday

Espresso profiles for cafés and home machines, followed by dialing sessions that confirm sweetness under pressure and milk.

Friday + Saturday

Second roast cycle for the week, restock of subscriptions, and public cuppings that let customers taste how origin and process shape the cup.
